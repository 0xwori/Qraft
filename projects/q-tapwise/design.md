# q-Tapwise Design Guide

Use this file when creating q-Tapwise designs, content, decks, social posts, or product notes.

This guide is based on the current Tapwise product, landing page, and frontend design references in the real Tapwise repo.

## Design Feeling

q-Tapwise should feel:

- warm, not cold
- educational, not generic SaaS
- clear, not decorative
- confident, not loud
- practical for real work

The style should help students, parents, teachers, and partners quickly understand what Tapwise does and why it is useful.

## Core Visual Rules

- Use a warm cream or stone background, close to `#faf8f6`.
- Use Tapwise orange `#fd6521` as the main brand accent.
- Use Geist Sans for headings, body text, labels, and UI.
- Use dark ink text on light warm surfaces.
- Use soft white or paper-like cards when content needs grouping.
- Prefer subtle borders and light shadows.
- Keep corners soft, usually 8px to 16px in product UI and up to 24px to 36px on landing-style cards.
- Use Lucide-style icons when icons are needed.

## Content Style

Write like Tapwise is a helpful learning product, not a flashy AI demo.

Good copy should be:

- simple
- concrete
- benefit-led
- easy for parents and students to understand
- focused on learning, exam prep, confidence, and saving time

Avoid:

- vague AI hype
- too many buzzwords
- overpromising results
- technical language when simple language works
- decorative sparkle language

## Landing Page Style

Landing-style work can be more spacious and emotional than app UI.

Use:

- big clear headings
- warm background
- one strong CTA
- glass-like or white cards when useful
- product screenshots, workflow mockups, or real examples
- soft motion if a frontend is being built

Do not use:

- neon gradients
- cold enterprise blue as the main look
- generic AI robot visuals
- crowded hero sections
- too many orange buttons

## Product UI Style

Product UI should feel like a calm learning workbench.

Use:

- dense but readable layouts
- stable navigation
- clear buttons
- readable forms
- compact status labels
- warm surfaces and dividers
- orange only for key actions, active states, or progress

Do not use nested cards or card-in-card layouts.

## Social And Ad Creative Style

For LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, and Meta ads:

- make the first message very clear
- show the learning benefit quickly
- use warm Tapwise colors
- keep one main idea per post or ad
- use parent/student language when the audience is students and parents
- include a clear next step

Example message angles:

- "Turn study material into practice."
- "Make exam prep easier to start."
- "Study from your own notes, not random material."
- "Less blank-page stress before a test."
